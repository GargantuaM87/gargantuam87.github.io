## Intro
Joseph Campbell once said, “Follow your bliss and the universe will open doors for where there were only walls.” A quote that is completely true as it is insightful; it’s like the law of attraction: what you truly pursue the most in life, you will be awarded opportunities to pursue it further. While experiencing the height of quarantine during middle school, I grew an obsession with Roblox games. Playing Roblox with my friends really helped me get through the tedious virtual classes and isolation rot. It wasn’t just playing games that interested me, but making them as well. I got interested in Roblox game development with 3D modeling in Blender, then I turned to LUA scripting, and then I began developing an interest in computer science. I went from AP Computer Science Principles and AP Computer Science A to majoring in Computer Science in college, and despite the current economic climate of this field, I couldn’t care less. Even before this unemployment crisis became a thing, I’ve had a strong interest in Computer Science, and I still will no matter what happens. 

Ok, so why do I feel so strongly about this study? Well, it’s simple. It makes me feel good, like *really* good. I really enjoy learning about the science of computers and hope to work in an industry that involves it. It’s something that I give great attention to, and it’s something that is a part of my identity. This is a passion that helps me love myself even more. The thing about passion is that in the pursuit of it, your life can feel much more satisfactory. 

Passions can make you feel more satisfied with life, give you more vitality, and help you define a purpose in life. Wait, before moving on, let’s address that last point. What really is the purpose of life? Is it about finding your purpose, or are you the purpose and you give it to life itself? It’s extremely subjective, and there will always be disagreements on the subject, but my personal definition is that life is so short and we’re all guaranteed to die someday, so it’s only natural to be the best version of ourselves before we die, so we can inspire the people that come after us. To me, living a passionate life is a great way to become the best version of yourself.
## Pursuing Passion
Alright, what exactly is passion? Well, it is usually an activity that induces positive thoughts and behavior whenever you think about it, or engage in it. According to a research article by [Elise L Rice and Barbara L Fredrickson](<https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9159683/>), “Specifically, harmonious passion has been linked to satisfaction with life (Carpentier, Mageau, & Vallerand, 2012; Rousseau & Vallerand, 2003 as described in Vallerand, 2010; Vallerand et al., 2007; Vallerand et al., 2008), meaning in life, and vitality (Rousseau & Vallerand, 2003), and experiences of flow.” You can find many examples of people enjoying their lives by loving what they do. Passion can also be described as a way of how you live your life, an important definition you should remember. Note that when I speak about passion, I exclude passion born of obsession, as it tends to induce more negative thoughts and can harm the person engaging in it. You can have a passion for cooking, gardening, drawing, *anything* that brings you joy through productive means. It doesn’t have to impress anyone or make you any money; the most important thing is that you want to pursue it because it makes you happy and helps define your identity. 

Now, let’s go over a few concepts that are important in pursuing your passions. These are three terms that I took from the book, “The Passion Test” by Janet Bray Attwood and Chris Attwood: **intention**, **attention**, and **no tension**. **Intention** is a way of consciously stating what you choose to create. You are being intentional with your actions, with your way of living, and to an extent, your thoughts. Many people fail at this aspect (sometimes not intentionally) when they’re so busy with mundane work that doesn’t align with what they want to do. It’s hard being intentional when everything else you’re doing is not by your intention, like trivial schoolwork. That’s why it’s important to give **attention** to what you want to create in your life. Your time may be limited amongst other duties, but when you’re truly passionate, you will find a way to give attention to that *one* or *many* passions that define your life. Fortunately, what you put your attention on grows stronger in your life, so the little time that you could only afford for video editing back then might be more abundant. As life is always changing, keeping your attention on your passions may become difficult. This is where **no tension** comes in. Having only intention and attention can make you rigid, but having no tension between yourself and what happens in your life promotes an open mind. Opening yourself up to how things appear rather than having set expectations can reduce the negative thoughts you have when something unexpected happens. Hey, you could explore new passions with an open mind, and maybe realize that the unfortunate event that happened to you recently happened for a reason. Intention, Attention, and No Tension are all important when pursuing your passions, and it’s as important to try to find something to pursue in the first place. Besides stimulating greater fulfillment in your life, thinking about the activities you're passionate about and how you want to live can deepen your critical thinking. You’re thinking outside the box to create a more gratifying life for yourself. This is the type of understanding and respect for yourself that you develop that proves your self-love.
## The Fear of Failure
Of course, pursuing your passions isn’t a simple guaranteed way to satisfy yourself, and even if it was, I doubt everyone would be doing it. Even if a particular person cares for a particular field of study, they probably wouldn’t pursue it if there’s a high chance of failure. What that failure entails is unknown, but the fact of the matter is that something can go wrong, and so many of us are afraid of the things that *can* go wrong. It’s this anxiety of failure ([atychiphobia](<https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diseases/22555-atychiphobia-fear-of-failure>)) that can sometimes make us predictable; if a person thinks they can’t do something, then they won’t do it. This ties into no tension, as having an open mind intuitively means you’re not afraid of failure or mistakes. Most of us know that failure and mistakes are a part of life, but just knowing that doesn’t get rid of the fear. The fear may stem from multiple factors and I aim to address a few of them. 

I’m a chronic overthinker. A lot of the time, I will dread past scenarios or be anxious about future events. Over the years, I’ve gotten a little better at directing my negative thoughts to the void, but I want to reminisce on one of the sources of these negative thoughts that I used to struggle with. **Comparison**. Whether you’re comparing yourself to a super star, that influencer on social media, a young and super rich business owner, that straight A’s student, or even someone who you think is beneath you (yes, that type of comparison induces negative thoughts even if it may make you feel good, it usually stems from a place of insecurity just like every other comparison), you’re coming from a place of fear. You may revere their success, but then you look at yourself and denounce yourself further. You criticize yourself for not having success as they do, but not just because you seemingly *can’t*, but because you *won’t*. You may be afraid of the effort you would have to put in, the time you would have to expend, or anything else you could lose. It’s crazy how so many of us will admire others' success, then look down on ourselves instead of telling ourselves that we can achieve that kind of success as well. When you think about it, this behavior comes from a place of ignorance as well. You don’t know exactly how that person met their success; you don’t know what they did, what they had, what they felt, what they heard, or what they saw. You don’t know Michael Jordan, you don’t know Christopher Nolan, you don’t know Usain Bolt. *You Don’t Know Them*. So it doesn’t make sense why you would compare yourself with others so distinct from yourself. We are our own persons, each one of us unique from each other. We can all reach success given the necessary time and effort, and that success looks different for every unique individual. 

Ok, let’s go back to this point, “You Don’t Know Them.” *And They Don’t Know You*. Only you know yourself. You know what you want to do and who you want to be, to an extent greater than anyone else can. Unfortunately, as we know, our minds will still find ways to trick us into believing something different, but don’t let it. Our minds are our greatest allies; do not let it antagonize you, and do not antagonize it. Focusing on failures is a habit our minds can become accustomed to, and it is extremely detrimental to productivity. But, if our minds can focus on the negative, then how about the positive? It’s certainly not easy if your life is *really* full of negativity, or if you have a mental disorder that makes it extremely difficult, yet, that doesn’t mean it’s impossible. Negativity can distort our reality through continuous belief in it; therefore, positivity should have a similar effect. I’m not saying to ignore the negative aspects of your life, but to rather keep the positive, wholesome, and better aspects of your life close to you, they can provide comfort and a means to keep moving no matter what. I started this section talking about how atychiphobia can prevent one from pursuing their passions, yet as one gets through that barrier, they can find that their existing passions serve as crutches whenever life knocks them down.  
## Conclusion
I strongly believe that living a passionate life means living a happier life. If you’re intent on what you want to create, determined to put attention towards it, and open yourself up to the way life is, you can eventually reach the success that you want to achieve without fear. Before I wrap this up, let me give you guys a few tips. Also taken from the book “Passion Test,” I present… the Passion Test. The procedure here is to write down at least 10 things that you are passionate about. These are things that describe how you want to live your life, such as “becoming a world-renowned actor” or “helping millions of people with affordable food.” These are things that you can get as passionate about as you want; you can worry about how you will pursue them later. Next, it’s important that you cull down that list of 10 or 15 to only 5 items. Through comparison, those 5 items should resonate with you the most. 

After you have your list, the next step is to think about the goals you use to pursue those grand passions. Note the difference between goals and passions: Passions are your favorite activities or ways of life that give you fulfillment, and goals are things you complete to move closer to living out your passions. As an example, if I had a passion to be “writing successful sci-fi novels,” then a goal might be to “publish my first sci-fi webnovel online.” You can see that your goals should align with your passions and clearly indicate that you’re making progress. This should all be a system that makes it easier to live out your passions. A good (and overly recommended) book that discusses systems, habits, and incremental improvements to achieve remarkable results is “Atomic Habits” by James Clear; it should be highly relevant to this topic. 

Now, that’s everything that I wanted to write about. Writing these journals provides a way for me to convey my thoughts, dissect them, and understand myself even deeper. Hopefully these thoughts can help someone. Now, get out there and live a passionate life, and remember these words from one of my favorite novels: “Do as you wish, but do no harm.”
